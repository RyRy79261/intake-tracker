# Insights redesign: methods and UX research

Sources: I used WebSearch for product and paper details (URLs inline). The algorithms and thresholds come from standard statistics and SPC knowledge, not from those searches; they are marked [knowledge].

## A. Methods for one person's sparse, irregular data

**Foundation [knowledge].** Aggregate to one value per day (or per AM/PM slot for BP) using the app's day-start hour, then work on that daily series. Keep `n` for every cell and never fill gaps with imputed values. Use robust statistics everywhere: median and MAD (`1.4826·median|x−med|`) instead of mean and SD. Two rules apply to every insight. (1) It must state n and the window. (2) It must pass a minimum-n gate, or it is not shown.

| # | Method | Question it answers | Minimal algorithm | Min n | Guards against false insights |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Personal baseline + deviation** | "Is today unusual *for me*?" | Baseline is the rolling median and MAD of the previous 28 days, excluding today. `z = (x−med)/MAD`. Flag when \|z\|>2.5. Oura and Apple use the same pattern: Oura learns averages for about 2 weeks and keeps a 2-month long-term average; Apple Trends compares the last 90 days with the last 365 | ≥14 days in the baseline, ≥7 of them with data | Use a lagged baseline so today cannot shift it. Use MAD so one bad day does not inflate the spread. Show the deviation only, with no causal claim |
| 2 | **EWMA control chart** | "Has my level drifted?" (slow drift that daily z misses) | `s_t = λx_t + (1−λ)s_{t−1}` with λ≈0.2. Limits are `med ± L·σ·sqrt(λ/(2−λ))` with L≈2.7 | 20 baseline points | Freeze the baseline after a confirmed shift. Autocorrelation narrows the limits, so estimate σ from the moving range (`MR̄/1.128`) |
| 3 | **CUSUM** | "Has a sustained shift started, and when?" | `S⁺ = max(0, S⁺ + (x−μ0)/σ − k)` with k=0.5. Alarm when S⁺>h, h≈4–5. Report the last time S⁺ was 0 as the estimated start | 20 | h controls the false-alarm rate (ARL₀≈370 at h=4.77, k=0.5). Reset after an alarm |
| 4 | **Change-point detection (offline)** | "Where did my weight or BP regime change?" | Binary segmentation or PELT, minimising the Gaussian mean-change cost plus a penalty β per change. Use BIC: β = 2·log(n)·σ². O(n²) is fine for n<1000 (Killick 2012, JASA 107:1590; PELT is O(n) expected) | ≥10 points per segment | Require a minimum segment length and a magnitude above the noise floor. Snap a change to a known med or titration date when it falls within ±3 days. Label it "possible" otherwise |
| 5 | **Before/after around medication changes (ITS)** | "What did the dose change do to BP, HR, weight or urination?" | For each dose change on date D, set a pre window of D−28…D−1 and a post window of D+L…D+L+27. Use L of 3–14 days, depending on the drug (washout). Fit segmented regression `y = b0 + b1·t + b2·post + b3·(t−D)·post`. The level change b2 is the headline. For the CI, use a block bootstrap (7-day blocks) or Newey-West errors. The simpler version is the difference of medians with a bootstrap CI | ≥8 measured days on each side | A pre-trend term catches regression to the mean, because doses are often changed *because* BP was high. Show the trend line. Exclude the lag window. Note co-occurring changes, such as another med or a salt change, and show "can't separate effects" when two changes are less than one window apart. N-of-1 literature names autocorrelation, trends and carryover as the main traps ([Daza](https://statsof1.netlify.com/2018_daza_latexversion.pdf), [arXiv 2208.00739](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.00739v4), [Lin 2016 bootstrap ITS](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/394089/2/Lin_et_al-2016-British_Journal_of_Mathematical_and_Statistical_Psychology.pdf)) |
| 6 | **Event-triggered average** | "BP the morning after >2 g salt days vs other days" | Split days by an exposure threshold, chosen in advance. Compare the outcome at lag k (morning of D+1) with median difference and a permutation or block-bootstrap CI. The same works for caffeine and BP or HR, alcohol and next-day weight, and fluid and urination count | ≥7 exposed and ≥7 unexposed days (Bearable's own guidance is 3–7 of each: [Bearable](https://bearable.app/support/troubleshooting/my-correlations-are-wrong-dont-make-sense/)) | Fix a small hypothesis list ahead of time from physiology: salt→BP, fluid→weight and urination, caffeine→HR, alcohol→BP, med→BP. Do not mine every pair. Control the rest with Benjamini–Hochberg at q=0.1. Match on day-of-week, or exclude weekends, to limit confounding |
| 7 | **Lagged cross-correlation** | "At what lag does X relate to Y?" | Prewhiten both series (differences or AR(1) residuals), then compute Spearman ρ for lags 0–3 days | ≥30 paired days | Without prewhitening, two trending series always correlate. With 4 lags × M pairs, apply BH. Show only \|ρ\|>0.3 with a CI that excludes 0. Exist.io limits itself to same-day and next-day ([Exist](https://kb.exist.io/article/55-will-exist-find-correlations-across-multiple-days)) |
| 8 | **Time-of-day / day-of-week profile** | "When is my BP highest? Do I drink less on weekends?" | Bin by hour, or by AM/PM for BP, and compute the 10/25/50/75/90th percentiles per bin. This is the AGP "modal day" ([Dexcom AGP](https://www.dexcom.com/faqs/what-is-the-agp-report)). For DOW, compare weekday medians with a Kruskal-Wallis test, or simply weekend vs weekday | ≥5 points per bin | Grey out bins with low n. Report only the largest contrast |
| 9 | **Consistency / time-in-range** | "How often am I on target?" | `% days in range = days within [lo, hi] / days with data`. Show it as a stacked bar (below / in / above), as in the CGM time-in-range consensus ([Battelino 2019 via Dexcom](https://www.dexcom.com/en-us/faqs/what-is-time-in-range)). Track streaks, longest streak, and logging coverage % separately | 7 days | Use days with data as the denominator, and always show coverage. A 100% figure over 3 logged days is misleading. Use a Wilson interval for the proportion |
| 10 | **Forecasting** | "When will I cross X kg? When do pills run out?" | Pills: `stock / (scheduled doses per day)`, which is deterministic and exact, minus any refill lead time. Weight: Theil-Sen slope over the last 28–42 days, then crossing date = (threshold − current)/slope. Get the CI by bootstrapping the slope, and show a date range. Suppress the forecast if the slope CI includes 0 | ≥14 weight days over ≥3 weeks | Do not extrapolate beyond 2× the fit window. Weight has water noise: use the 7-day median, not single weigh-ins |
| 11 | **Quantile reporting** | Replace "average 1.8 L" | Report the median plus a typical range (IQR, or P10–P90). Add "most days between X and Y" | 7 | Means hide skew. For BP, follow home-monitoring protocol: AM+PM duplicate readings over ≥3–7 days for a report value ([RACGP](https://www.racgp.org.au/afp/2016/january-february/how-to-measure-home-blood-pressure-recommendations); evidence that discarding day 1 is unnecessary: [PubMed 19809360](https://www.pubmed.gov/19809360/)) |

**Cross-cutting guards [knowledge]**
- **Pre-registered insight catalogue.** Define about 15–25 insight templates, each with a fixed hypothesis, threshold, lag and minimum n. This avoids a garden of forking paths and caps the number of comparisons.
- **Autocorrelation.** Use a block bootstrap with 7-day blocks, or an effective-n correction `n_eff = n·(1−r1)/(1+r1)`. Use n_eff in every minimum-n gate.
- **Regression to the mean.** Never headline "your worst week improved". Compare against a baseline that existed before the trigger.
- **Stability.** Show an insight only if it holds in both halves of the window, or persists across 2 consecutive weekly recomputations. This cuts flicker.
- **Logging bias.** People log BP more when they feel unwell. Show logging frequency next to the effect.

**Expressing uncertainty to a layperson [knowledge]**
- Use a sentence with a number and a range: "Your morning systolic was **6 mmHg higher** (likely 2–10) after salty days. Based on 11 salty and 34 other days."
- Use 3 confidence levels (Strong / Likely / Early hint), mapped from CI width and n. Exist uses 1–5 stars that grow with data ([Exist](https://kb.exist.io/article/37-what-are-correlations)).
- Always add "pattern, not proof" and the confounders checked.
- When data is insufficient, show progress, such as "4 more salty-day readings needed", not a guess.

## B. Insight UX patterns

**What makes an insight useful**
1. **Compared with *your* baseline, not population norms.** Oura scores contributors against personal averages ([Oura](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/360057791533)), and Apple Trends compares 90 days with 365 ([leancrew](https://leancrew.com/all-this/2024/11/apple-health-trends/)).
2. **"What changed", not "what is".** Lead with deltas, direction and a date.
3. **Few insights.** Apple deliberately limited the number of trends shown ([9to5mac interview](https://9to5mac.com/2021/06/16/apple-execs-apple-health-watch-watch-interview/)).
4. **A plain sentence with one number**, plus a sparkline that shows the evidence. Tapping it drills down to the underlying days.
5. **Honest about causality.** Bearable and Exist both say this explicitly ([Bearable](https://bearable.app/support/troubleshooting/my-correlations-are-wrong-dont-make-sense/)). Visible's users complain when a score lags their symptoms or is opaque ([Visible](https://www.makevisible.com/blog/introducing-the-morning-stability-score)). That argues for showing the inputs.
6. **Actionable or clinically legible.** Time-in-range targets, and reports a doctor recognises (AGP, ESH-style home-BP averages, Omron/Kardia PDF reports).

**What makes it noise:** unconditioned averages, every-pair correlations, scores without explanation, insights that flip week to week, and absolute claims from a few days of data.

**Five structurally different layouts**
1. **Ranked insight feed.** Cards are ranked by |effect|×confidence×recency, and each one has a sentence, a sparkline, a confidence chip and "See evidence". Models: Apple Highlights, Bearable Factor Effect. Good for discovery, but it needs strict gating or it becomes noise.
2. **"What changed" diff (this week vs your baseline).** A table of metrics with a baseline range, this period, a Δ arrow and a flag. It shows only rows outside the normal band, and at the top, events such as a dose change or new symptom. Models: Apple Trends, Oura contributors. Fast to scan, and it doubles as a weekly check-in.
3. **Condition report (AGP-style one-pager).** A fixed, standardised layout: time-in-range bars for fluid, salt and BP, a modal-day percentile band for BP and HR, a daily strip of small multiples, a medication timeline and the journal symptoms. Exports to a PDF for the doctor. Models: AGP / Dexcom Clarity ([Dexcom](https://www.dexcom.com/faqs/what-is-the-agp-report)) and Omron BP reports. Trustworthy because nothing in it is "discovered".
4. **Annotated timeline.** One shared time axis with stacked lanes: BP, weight, fluid and salt, urination. Medication phases appear as shaded bands, with change-points and journal entries as pins. Each dose change has a before/after card (method A5). This fits titration best.
5. **Question-driven explorer.** The user picks from preset questions, such as "Does salt raise my BP next morning?", "Did the new dose work?" or "When will I run out?". Each opens a fixed analysis with a verdict, CI and evidence. Claude can write the narrative from the computed numbers only. Models: Bearable comparison graph, Exist.

**Suggested combination:** use layout 2 as the default view, with layout 4 one tap away, layout 3 as the export, and layout 1 restricted to 3–5 gated cards. Claude should get only computed statistics, never raw records, and is asked to paraphrase without adding claims.
